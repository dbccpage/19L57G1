# Chances and responses: practice answers

Try the [practice](VALUATIONS_PRACTICE.md) before this guide.

1. No state: the preparation chances total 4/3. Yes effect: 1,1 gives
   Yes with certainty at either outcome. Its cross-outcome sum need not be one.
2. Contributions are `2/3 × 1/3 = 2/9` and `1/3 × 1 = 1/3`.
   Yes chance is 5/9 and complementary No chance is 4/9.
3. The resulting table is `(1/3,2/3)`. Mixture weights totaling 4/3
   are refused, not silently renormalized.
4. Independent equal choices give Yes chance 1/2. Coordinated matching
   choices give one; coordinated opposite choices give zero. The joint
   choice distributions differ despite identical separate mixture shares.
5. Both give `(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)`. A response depending only on this
   table has equal valuation on both. A record of recipe labels would
   be additional information, not another table-only response.
6. The two distinct indicator weights must be 1/3 and 2/3. This is
   uniqueness over those candidates; it does not recover historical labels,
   repeated recipes or a physical preparation mechanism.
7. The whole B=0 chance is 1/2. The joint A=0/B=0 chance is also 1/2.
   Conditional on A=0, divide that joint chance by the A=0 chance 1/2,
   giving one. The whole-list marginal and selected-branch chance differ.
8. None: division by its zero chance is not supplied. A missing record
   does not establish zero chance, so omitting an unrecorded real branch
   lacks that justification. State the actual selection/missing-data rule.
