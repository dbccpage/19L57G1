# 41. Two recipes, one table

Now restrict the allowed family to mixtures of four supplied corner states:

| Corner | Outcome 0 | Outcome 1 | Outcome 2 | Outcome 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | 1/2 | 0 | 1/2 | 0 |
| V2 | 0 | 1/2 | 1/2 | 0 |
| V3 | 0 | 1/2 | 0 | 1/2 |
| V4 | 1/2 | 0 | 0 | 1/2 |

**Predict:** compare an equal mixture of V1/V3 with an equal mixture of
V2/V4. Do they give different outcome tables?

<details><summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Both give `(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4)`. Their corner weights are different:
`(1/2,0,1/2,0)` versus `(0,1/2,0,1/2)`. The outcome table does not
identify which of those two recipes was used.

</details>

This is P204's **square state space**, meaning the allowed convex family
generated by these four corners. It is a restriction of four-outcome
probability tables. The name describes this supplied family, not a claim
that every physical state space has this shape.

The corners are extreme relative to this restricted family. For V1,
the zero at outcome 1 excludes V2/V3 from a nonnegative mixture; the
zero at outcome 3 excludes V3/V4. Only V1 can remain. The other corners
have the corresponding zero-coordinate argument. The optional source
guide records the book's common-denominator proof of that reasoning.

In the larger family allowing all four outcome indicators, each of these
corners is itself a mixture of two indicators. “Extreme” therefore needs
the allowed family. The center's indicator weights are uniquely 1/4 each,
even though its corner weights above are not unique.

**Change one thing:** mix all four corners equally. What table results?

<details><summary>Check the changed example</summary>

Again the center. It supplies a third corner-weight recipe, not a third
outcome table. All response rules that depend only on that table give
the same valuation; recipe-history questions need additional data.

</details>
