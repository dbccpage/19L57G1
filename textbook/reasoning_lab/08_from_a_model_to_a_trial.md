## Predict first

Our supplied model table contains no card with dot Yes and crease No.
Does that force a real paper card to behave the same way?

## Fix the proposed correspondence

No measurement has been performed in these lessons. The following logs
are **supplied fiction** for learning how to interpret evidence.

We propose an application to four paper cards, K, L, M, N:

> Under protocol P1 at the front of the card on the desk, if a card has
> a dot, then it has a crease.

P1 asks two separate questions about a marked answer box:

- **Dot:** is a filled ink spot clearly visible entirely inside the box?
- **Crease:** is a fold line clearly visible crossing the box?

Write Y for a clear Yes, N for a clear No, and ? for an unclear or
unrecorded answer. Inspect each feature; do not infer the crease answer
from the dot answer. Fix the front, lighting and procedure before the trial.

These choices define how we intend to record an observation. They do
not make the proposed if-then rule true of the cards.

## Four different jobs

| Statement | Its job |
|---|---|
| “Y is the code for Yes under P1.” | Definition of the recording convention |
| “A Yes/No pair conflicts with the if-then rule.” | Deduction under that interpretation |
| “The supplied packet has one in-scope counterexample.” | Finite calculation on fictional logs |
| “A person inspected N and recorded Y/N under P1.” | A measurement report, if actually performed |

A sentence's job does not authenticate it. A fictional sentence about
an inspection remains fictional; a real report still needs its source
and conditions.
