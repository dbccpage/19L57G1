# 04. Every and some

[Previous](03_check_both_directions.md) · [Reasoning lab](README.md) · [Next](05_order_of_questions.md)

## Predict first

A is round. Does that establish “some listed card is round”? Does it
establish “every listed card is round”?

## Say how many cases the sentence covers

**Some** means at least one, not exactly one. A is a witness for “some
listed card is round”: name A and show its Yes answer. B is another
witness for “some listed card is blue”; two witnesses do not spoil “some.”

**Every** means each member of the declared collection. One No answer
refutes “every listed card is round.” B or C supplies a counterexample.
One successful example does not establish an every-claim.

On this finite list, we can check every row. Checking A and B while
leaving C unexamined would not check the whole list. Even checking all
three does not check an unlimited collection of future cards.

## What if we select no cards?

The desk lets us declare an empty list. “Some selected card is blue”
then fails: there is no card to supply a witness.

“Every selected card is blue” passes the no-counterexample check, because
there is no selected card with a No answer. This empty-domain convention
does not produce a blue card or establish that blue cards exist.

Likewise an if-then claim can have no counterexamples even when its first
condition holds on no selected card. The desk reports that count so a
reader can distinguish this situation from successful positive examples.

## Your turn

Select only C. Compare “every selected card is round” with “if a selected
card is round, then it is blue.” Explain the different results.
