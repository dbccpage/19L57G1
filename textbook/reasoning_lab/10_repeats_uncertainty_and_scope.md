## Predict first

Continue the fictional log for card N under P1/front-desk:

| Trial | Dot | Crease |
|---|---|---|
| new-N | Y | N |
| repeat-N | Y | Y |
| unclear-N | Y | ? |

Does the second trial erase the first? Does ? mean No?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Neither. Preserve all three rows. The first and second give conflicting
clear crease answers under the recorded conditions. The third does not
supply a clear crease answer. An uncertain reading must not be turned
into the convenient answer for a preferred rule.

</details>

## Which input does the function use?

A deterministic table assigns one output to each input. If only the card
is the input, it cannot assign both clear crease answers to N. Check whether the procedure, conditions, card or
recording assumptions need revision. Indexing by trial instead gives
different inputs, so a function can assign different outputs—but the
new index does not explain why the readings differ.

We have not supplied a rule for how often readings should vary. The
disagreement does not supply such a rule automatically.

## Partial answers can still settle a conditional

| Dot | Crease | Status of “if dot, then crease” for this row |
|---|---|---|
| Y | N | Counterexample under the stated interpretation |
| Y | ? | Unresolved |
| N | ? | Compatible: the first condition is No |
| ? | Y | Compatible: the required second answer is Yes |
| ? | ? | Unresolved |

“Compatible” means this row supplies no counterexample. It does not
establish the rule for every card or every future trial.

## Scope is part of the claim

A Yes/No row about card O is outside the fixed K/L/M/N domain. A row
under P2 requires its own procedure and interpretation. A back-view
reading is outside the front-desk conditions. Retain these rows while
keeping their status explicit; do not silently enlarge the claim or
discard inconvenient in-scope rows.
