# 44. From chance tables to observations

A model predicts Yes chance 5/8. A team runs eight trials, observes five
Yes answers, and concludes that the supplied preparation and response
tables have been proved exactly correct.

**Predict:** does that one count determine both tables?

<details><summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. Different preparation/response pairs can give the same overall
valuation. A finite count also need not equal its model chance exactly.
To assess a device model, retain the procedure, data and uncertainty
rather than identifying one observed ratio with an exact law.

</details>

The table arithmetic helps state a testable proposal. It does not choose
its own preparation protocol, authenticate the records or supply a rule
for judging discrepancies. Those require additional, explicitly stated work.

**Change one thing:** the overall chance agrees, but the individual
preparation tables differ. What can still be reported?

<details><summary>Check the changed example</summary>
