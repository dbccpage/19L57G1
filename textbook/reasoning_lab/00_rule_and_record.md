# 00. A rule and a record

[Reasoning lab](README.md) · [Next](01_one_answer_per_input.md)

## Predict first

The table says that A answers Yes to **blue?**. Does that one entry tell
us what every other card must answer?

| Card | blue? | round? | triangle? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Yes | Yes | No |
| B | Yes | No | Yes |
| C | No | No | Yes |

These are supplied answers. For this exercise the table is complete.
We obtain an answer by reading the named row and column.

## Keep three jobs separate

A **record** states a particular entry: A answers Yes to **blue?**.

A **rule** tells us how a permitted step works: to answer **blue?** about
a listed card, read that card's entry in the blue column.

A **definition** fixes how we use a word: in this lab a claim “holds on
the list” when no listed card violates it. A definition fixes a task;
it does not establish that a particular claim passes the task.

The table and reading rule were supplied. Reading them does not explain
how color could be measured or why a physical card has that color.

## An attempted shortcut

“A is blue, so C must be blue” does not follow from this record or rule.
It also disagrees with C's supplied answer. To justify a step, identify
the rule and the information it requires.
