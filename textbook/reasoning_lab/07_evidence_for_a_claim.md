# 07. Match evidence to a claim

[Previous](06_carry_the_hypotheses.md) · [Reasoning lab](README.md) · [Practice](PRACTICE.md)

## Predict first

A program reads our supplied table and reports that round implies blue.
Has it established how an actual parcel sensor behaves tomorrow?

## Ask what was checked

| Kind of statement | What would support this example? |
|---|---|
| Definition | An explicit choice of what “holds on the list” means |
| Consequence of stated assumptions | A valid argument using those assumptions |
| Finite computation | Reproducible calculation covering the named table or population |
| Claim about a device | Relevant measurements and a checked interpretation of its readings |

A definition sets a meaning. An argument establishes its conclusion
under its hypotheses. A computation establishes what that calculation
checked. Measurements concern the actual measured cases and conditions.
These jobs can cooperate, but evidence for one does not automatically do
all the others.

## A concrete next question

Suppose a parcel scanner labels items round and blue. Record exactly
what those labels mean operationally: which procedure produces each
answer, on which parcels, under which conditions?

The table's conditional predicts no round-Yes, blue-No case in its domain.
If the scanner produces that combination, inspect the correspondence:
does the parcel belong to the declared domain, are the procedures the
ones specified, and does the proposed rule agree with their readings?

Repeated or uncertain readings need a model that represents those
possibilities. Our complete two-answer table has no noise or missing-data
field. It cannot settle such questions by itself.
