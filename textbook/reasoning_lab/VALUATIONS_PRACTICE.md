# Chances and responses: changed practice

Attempt these after Lessons 37–44, before opening the separate
[answers](VALUATIONS_ANSWERS.md). All tables are supplied examples.

1. Do preparation chances 2/3,2/3 define a state on two outcomes?
   Does the response rule “always Yes,” with weights 1,1, define an effect?
2. Use preparation chances 2/3,1/3 and response chances 1/3,1. Calculate
   the two contributions, overall Yes chance and complementary No chance.
3. Mix certain-outcome-0 and certain-outcome-1 recipes with weights 1/3
   and 2/3. Give the resulting preparation table. What if the two recipe
   weights instead sum to 4/3?
4. Mix those two certain recipes equally, and mix the two matching
   indicator effects equally. Compare independent recipe/response choices
   with coordinated matching choices and coordinated opposite choices.
5. For the four corners in Lesson 41, compare equal V1/V3 and equal
   V2/V4 mixtures. Can a table-only response distinguish those recipes?
6. For a binary classical simplex, which indicator weights represent
   `(1/3,2/3)`? Does that recover a physical recipe's history?
7. In the equal 00/11 joint table, compare the whole-list B=0 chance,
   the joint A=0/B=0 chance, and B=0 conditional on A=0. Which numerator
   and denominator does the last answer use?
8. Use an A partition with one certain branch and one impossible branch.
   What conditional chance does the impossible branch supply? Would
   omitting a real but unrecorded branch have the same justification?
