# 36. From parts to a device claim

A team draws the three-edge chain from Lesson 33. It concludes:
“A change at A is physically detectable at B immediately, because
the graph connects them.”

**Predict:** which earlier distinctions does that conclusion skip?

<details><summary>Check your prediction</summary>

An available rule was substituted for an executed rule. Undirected
connectivity was substituted for a direction and timing claim. No
correspondence between registers, updates and actual devices was supplied.
The chosen readout might also hide the change.

</details>

The model gives useful conditional predictions. An actual A-only update
keeps B's fields; a following relay can copy an altered A value into B;
the readout and preparation determine whether that change is visible.
These statements help design a comparison. They do not replace it.

For an independently chosen device experiment, retain the following
blank record. Generated desk rows are not entries for the observed fields.

Comparing a changed run with a baseline that omits the relay would
mix the effect of the A update with the effect of the relay itself.
Keep that baseline explicit, as the desk does for both relay procedures.

**Change one thing:** observed outcome shares agree, but every paired
reading differs. Which claim must you withdraw?

<details><summary>Check the changed example</summary>

Withdraw “each reading was unchanged.” Equal shares for that readout
can still be reported. They do not establish no influence, equal complete
states or agreement for every other readout.

</details>
