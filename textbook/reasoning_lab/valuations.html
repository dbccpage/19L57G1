How do the chances combine?

A preparation gives a chance to each outcome. A response rule gives a Yes chance if that outcome occurs. Multiply each pair, then add the contributions.

Why the response weights need not sum to one

They answer different conditional questions: the Yes chance if outcome 0 occurs, and the Yes chance if outcome 1 occurs. “Always yes” has response weights 1,1. Its overall Yes chance is still one because preparation shares sum to one. The complementary response has weights one minus each original weight.

2. Keep both A branches

Keeping one branch asks another question

A branch's joint Yes contribution includes its chance of occurring. Dividing that contribution by the branch chance gives the Yes chance conditional on keeping that branch. A zero-chance branch supplies no such ratio. Conditional chances can differ from the answer obtained by retaining all branches; that does not contradict the full-branch sum identity.
