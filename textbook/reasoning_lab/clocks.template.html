Which changes do the clocks count?

1. Predict an interval ratio

Both models start with A=0, B=3 and counters 0,0. Python adds 1 to fast on every update and 1 to slow on alternate updates; B cycles through 0,1,2,3. Lean instead adds 2 to fast, 1 to slow and 1 to B on every update. Both add 1 to A. These are different declared rules.

This is a separate supplied countdown model, not either clock rule. A counter drops by one per update and stays at zero. Its lamp reads 1 at zero and 0 otherwise. Counter A starts at H and B at H+1. We show H samples, at indices 0 through H−1.

Why equal current registers do suffice in the specific Lean model

Lean's P202 readout contains only A and B. Their next values depend only on their current values, not on either clock. The book proves that equal current readouts give equal readouts at every future index in this model. Different clock fields can remain invisible to that readout. The separate countdown changes the update and what is read; it does not contradict that theorem.
