# Practice: supports and withdrawal

[Start at Lesson 62](62_keep_the_support.md) · [Answers after your attempt](REVISION_ANSWERS.md)

Use a new fictional notebook about one lamp at one time. Keep two fixed
rules: if P, then Q; if Q, then R. Interpret P as “button pressed,” Q as
“contact closed,” and R as “lamp lit.” Hypothesis a says P; hypothesis b
says not-R. Explicitly record Q from P and R from Q.

1. List the hypothesis support of each fixed rule, Q and R. Which fixed
   assumptions remain outside those hypothesis lists?
2. Name the conflict support. Give every minimum withdrawal when both
   costs are 1. Which candidate does the default name-order selector choose?
3. If a is withdrawn, list the hypothesis and derived records deactivated.
   Does the operation assert not-P or not-Q?
4. In a fresh copy, give a cost 4 and b cost 1. Which withdrawal wins?
   Does its lower cost establish which lamp report is accurate?
5. Add hypothesis c saying Q and record a second R from it. Name the two
   conflict supports. Does withdrawing a alone suffice? Find the withdrawal
   with the fewest names, at equal costs.
6. Separately, suppose the conflict supports are a/b and a/c, with costs
   a=9, b=1, c=1. Compare withdrawing a with withdrawing b and c together
   using the declared order.
7. In a fresh notebook, record P, the rule if-P-then-Q, and not-Q, but
   do not yet record any derived Q. Does an empty direct-conflict scan
   establish that no conflict could be derived? What changes after Q is recorded?
8. Two fixed assumptions directly say Q and not-Q, with no hypotheses
   involved. Can hypothesis withdrawal repair that conflict in this model?
9. Suppose two independent records say Q, one supported by a and another
   by c. If a is withdrawn, must both records be deactivated?
10. A lamp notebook has no detected conflict. Give one reason its surviving
    report could still be false about a real lamp, and one observation that
    could bear on that possibility. Do not describe an unperformed check
    as evidence already obtained.

AuthorityEffect: None. These cases are supplied, not observations.
