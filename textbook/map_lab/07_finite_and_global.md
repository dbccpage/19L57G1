## A condition over this domain

The finite recovery statement names a particular domain. It requires
the lookup equations only for that domain's members. Adding an excluded
card can introduce a new conflict. The result on the original list has
not become false; a stronger statement on a larger list has failed.

On the empty domain every lookup passes the equations because no card
needs checking. That establishes no card, no observed answer, and no
successful prediction for an unlisted case.

## A different, global statement

The native observer-quotient result requires agreement on every pair
identified by its specified observer across that observer's whole carrier.
Its carrier might itself be finite, as in the book's four-card example.
“Global” names the whole declared carrier, not the physical universe.

The source connects a global natural-number observer to factorization
over **every** represented finite domain. One selected domain cannot
replace that “every.” The source even gives an empty-domain counterexample
to show why the quantifier matters.
