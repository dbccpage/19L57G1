## Source and claim boundaries

Python adds one fast tick and a slow tick on alternate step indices;
B cycles modulo four. Lean adds two fast ticks, one slow tick and one
to B each step. These are different transitions. Python's three-step
fast/slow change ratio is 3 from index 0 and 3/2 from index 1. An
even-length example does not prove a constant ratio over every interval.
The source ledger and receipt remain unchanged; the route states the
narrower inspected scope.

The native Lean theorem proves `fast change = 2 × slow change`.
The book establishes positive slow change when k>0 and distinguishes
zero-change algebra from a defined quotient. A 10/3 origin reaching
14/5 has elapsed changes 4/2; final totals are not the elapsed ratio.

The native readout contains A/B but excludes clocks. A new book theorem
proves that, for this particular Lean transition, equal current A/B
readouts characterize all-future readout equivalence. Different clocks
remain invisible; no complete-state or enlarged-observer equality follows.

A separate countdown starting at H and H+1 agrees on the first H lamp
samples and differs at H. The book proves this for every Nat H. It
limits unrestricted finite-to-all-future inference without pretending
the countdown is a P202 trajectory or contradicts the model-specific
characterization. A bounded shifted-start theorem retains the H+1-to-H
comparison allowance.

Raw Python zero/negative horizons yield empty trajectories. The book
rejects negatives and explains empty agreement. Caller checks name the
registers, nonnegative fields and fixed periods. The raw rate helper
checks neither replay nor interval direction; the desk replays its rule
to obtain endpoints. Lean-rule execution is an explicit book adapter,
with arithmetic checks and separate Lean proofs, not a refinement theorem.
