# Teaching costs, potentials, and allowed path changes

Date: 2026-10-31. AuthorityEffect: None.

The existing Path lab supplied signed boundaries, but not a worked route
from a step cost to the distinction between closed and exact. Lessons
07–12 add six short explanations, a two-cost return-loop diagram, separate
changed-cost practice and answers, and two prediction panels. The new
material explicitly changes from the vertex/face calculus to the native
passage state and event machine.

## Owner review

The current `native_structure` README, ledger, Python cost functions, and
Cells, Boundary and Closed Lean bodies were read. The continuation teaches
selected NS-1/4/6/7/8/10: actual-start commutation, signed path costs,
potentials, return-loop obstruction, closed-but-not-exact cost, and
preservation under verified swaps. It does not teach the proof-relevant
cell calculus, observer descent, action/charge question, or the separate
conservation carrier. `cost_cohomology`'s finite graph-rank claim is not
silently imported as a potential theorem.

Definitions are separated into small questions. A step count distinguishes
attempted events from state-changing steps. A loop requires the whole end
state to equal its start. A commuting square requires equal full ends
before its two cost totals are compared. A zero signed-budget reading on
the noncommuting halt/license comparison is retained, so equality of one
observable number is not mistaken for commutation. Exactness is defined
by a potential on every relevant step, rather than by absence of a failure
in a small sample.

The source's state-change count is both closed on every commuting square
and not exact. The general proof is re-elaborated; the bounded desk merely
illustrates it. A verified swap preserves events and their number, so its
cost theorem does not authorize deleting a nonzero loop. These meanings
of `Cell` and square are kept separate from the earlier face calculus.
