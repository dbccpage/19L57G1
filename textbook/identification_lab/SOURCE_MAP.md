## Declared choices and finite calculations

The two source histories are g00 and g05 from
`research/identification_dual/receipts/identification_dual.json`.
`run_lab.py` decodes and replays their events, then compares their exact
admitted regimes with the summary. The teaching names A/B/C denote their
indices 0/1/2, not new admissions. Both histories use the source's F
admission reading. The independent keep/release choice is its P/R
requirement-retention axis; the letters describe different choices.

Each root calculation selects one of three pairs, keep or release, and
one of the two source measures. New forms have arity at most two; replies
contain at most two items. Candidates are the sealed **unpruned**
well-formed addition enumeration at item counts zero, one, and two,
deduplicated, omitting already-active uniform-rule items as the research
minimum function does. The packet records the candidate population for
each probe. Candidate coverage relies on that existing enumerator.

For each candidate, the teaching check independently builds all allowed
slot tuples, directly plugs data into them, and checks instance/rule
membership. It compares the resulting table and required equal/unequal
rows with the actual profile and response APIs. Minimum prices and all
tied deltas are compared with `dual.min_responses`. The source's full
state isomorphism key groups tied replies; equal answer tables alone
do not certify state isomorphism. The checker does not independently
prove completeness of that key or the candidate generator.

The first measure counts items. The second compares `(items, sum of
new-form arities)` in that order. The source's internal `-1` sentinel
for the first measure is displayed as a scalar item count, not a
negative price. No general efficiency or effort interpretation follows.

## Deliberately limited treatment

This route does not rerun the research's four baseline trees or its full
identification campaign, authenticate an independent discovery, prove
the open general bounds, or add a physical identity/merge primitive.
The original prediction that all never-required pairs would answer under
the retaining setting failed in seven finite binary states, including
g05. We teach one such refusal, rather than repeating the prediction as
a theorem. Native ID-13–ID-15 conjectures remain conjectures.

The figures' spacing, colors, letters, and arrow drawings are teaching
choices. Reader feedback supports readability but does not supply a
scored novice transfer or delayed-retention result.
