# Practice: change the table

Use three new cards X, Y, and Z, with two one-place forms P and Q.
Only P(X) and Q(Y) are admitted. There are no uniform rules and no
old separation promises.

| Card | P | Q |
|---|---|---|
| X | yes | — |
| Y | — | yes |
| Z | — | — |

A dash means not derivable in this record. Each new instance or
uniform rule costs one item. A uniform rule makes its form hold on
all admitted cards. The full two-item search also permits new data
and forms, with new forms having at most two places.

1. Name a question witnessing that X and Y are currently different.
2. Match X and Y by adding instances only. How many items did you use?
   Explain why zero items or one item cannot work in this example.
3. Find all replies of at most two items that match X and Y. Compare
   Z's row after each one. Which replies leave Z unchanged?
4. If we additionally promise to keep Z distinguishable from X and Y,
   which of those replies remain valid?
5. Your program reports no reply within a two-item budget for some
   other state. What extra claim would be needed to say there is no
   reply at any budget?
6. A friend says: “X and Y now give equal answers, so the model has
   deleted one card.” What part of the record would you inspect?

These are supplied book questions, not additional research-campaign
states. The [answers](ANSWERS.md) show the complete bounded results
for this practice table. Try writing your reasons first.
