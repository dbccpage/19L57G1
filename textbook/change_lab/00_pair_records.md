# 00. Three separate pair records

[Change lab](README.md) · [Next: observation](01_declare_an_observation.md)

## Predict before adding a record

We admit three entry tokens: 10, 20, 30. Their positions are 0, 1, 2,
which we call A, B, C. The numbers identify supplied tokens and positions;
they are not measured properties of objects.

Now someone asserts a difference between B and C. Has the machine also
kept it as a retained pair? Has it committed to distinguish the pair?

## Three additions with three effects

| Addition | Resulting record | What it does not supply |
|---|---|---|
| Assert B/C | The assertion is listed | Retention, protection, or a distinguishing test |
| Admit the asserted pair | B/C is retained | Protection or a distinguishing test |
| Protect the retained pair | B/C carries a demand to remain distinguishable | A test that meets the demand |

An attempted retention before assertion changes nothing. An attempted
protection before retention changes nothing. These are guards of this
declared machine; the guards themselves were supplied.

## The word “witness” needs its setting

The passage source calls its retained pair store `wit`, short for witness.
We call its entries **retained pair records** here. Recording one does
not prove that the current observation distinguishes its endpoints.

In First steps, a **separating-test witness** was a particular test and
its different answers. That is a different role. A retained pair record
can exist before any observation has even been declared.

Neither kind of formal record, by itself, proves a fact about an actual
physical item. A real application needs a tested measurement interpretation.

## The ledger for our worked case

We assert and retain A/B, A/C, and B/C. We initially protect A/B and B/C.
A/C is retained but not yet protected. These choices will let us see
exactly what changes when we later protect A/C too.
