# 03. Use signed steps

Choose a zero point on a line. Count rightward steps as positive and
leftward steps as negative. The number **−2** means two steps left of
zero. It is not a negative count of counters in a tray; it belongs
to a different numerical setting.

To add signed steps, follow one instruction and then the next.
Three steps right followed by two left ends one step right:
**3 + (−2) = 1**. Predict where two left followed by one left ends.

It ends three left: **−2 + (−1) = −3**. A positive and an equally
large negative cancel: **2 + (−2) = 0**.

For multiplication we use the admitted integer rules. Multiplying by
a positive whole number repeats the signed step. Multiplying by −1
reverses its direction. Thus 2 × (−2) = −4, while
(−1) × (−2) = 2. In general the product of two negatives is positive.
These are mathematical rules we are using, not laws deduced from the
card model.

To *square* a number, multiply it by itself. We write 2² for 2 × 2.
Both **2² = 4** and **(−2)² = 4**. Parentheses keep the signed number
together before it is squared. Also 0² = 0.

Knowing the square alone therefore need not tell you the original
direction. If the original number is restricted to nonnegative whole
numbers, that ambiguity disappears. The allowed domain matters.
