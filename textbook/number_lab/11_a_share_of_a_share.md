# 11. A share of a share

Shade 3/4 of a rectangle. Now keep half of that shaded part.

**Predict:** have you kept 1/2 of the whole rectangle, or half of 3/4?

<details><summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Half of 3/4. Splitting every quarter in half produces eighths; the
three shaded quarters contribute three retained eighths.
Thus **1/2 × 3/4 = 3/8** of the original whole.

</details>

Multiplying fractions takes a share of a share. Multiply the numerators
for the retained piece count and the denominators for the finer piece
size. For example:

**2/3 × 9/10 = 18/30 = 3/5**.

The intermediate 18/30 and the reduced 3/5 name the same value. Reducing
the fraction does not change the quantity.

This operation helps explain a response calculation. If a supplied
model gives an outcome chance 3/4 and a Yes chance 1/2 **when that
outcome occurs**, its contribution to overall Yes is 3/8. The second
chance is conditional on the first outcome; it is not another unrelated
3/4-to-1/2 comparison.

Use parentheses to state which operation happens first. In
**(1/2 + 1/4) × 2/3**, combine the bracketed shares first, then take
2/3 of 3/4, giving 1/2.

**Change one thing:** use **1/2 + (1/4 × 2/3)** instead. Is its value
still 1/2?

<details><summary>Check the changed example</summary>

No. The product inside the brackets is 1/6. Adding 1/2 gives
3/6 + 1/6 = 4/6 = 2/3. Moving the brackets changed the specified calculation.

</details>
