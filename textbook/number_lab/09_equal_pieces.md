# 09. Equal pieces and the same share

Use [Number Lessons 00–02](00_count_and_multiply.md) first. For this
fraction branch, you can move here directly from Lesson 02.

Cut one rectangle into four equal pieces and shade three. The shaded
share is **3/4**: three pieces, each one quarter of the same whole.
The bottom number is the **denominator**; it tells the piece size. The
top number is the **numerator**; it counts those pieces.

**Predict:** cut every quarter into two equal pieces. Does the shaded
amount grow? What fraction now describes it?

<details><summary>Check your prediction</summary>

The amount stays the same. There are now eight equal pieces, six shaded:
**3/4 = 6/8**. We doubled both numbers, not the shaded amount.

</details>

Multiplying numerator and denominator by the same positive integer
preserves the share. Reversing that step reduces a fraction when both
numbers have a common factor. Thus 6/8 can be written as 3/4.
The two written records differ while naming the same value.

The denominator must be positive in this toolkit. “Three zero-sized
equal parts of this whole” does not supply a fraction 3/0. Zero in the
numerator is different: 0/4 describes no shaded quarters and has value zero.

**Change one thing:** compare 1/4 with 1/8. The numerators match;
do the values match?

<details><summary>Check the changed example</summary>

No. One quarter is two eighths of the same whole. Piece size matters,
so matching only the numerator is insufficient.

</details>

Keep the same whole when comparing shares. A quarter of a large sheet
need not have the same area as a quarter of a smaller sheet.
