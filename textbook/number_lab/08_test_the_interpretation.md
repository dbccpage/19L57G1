# 08. Test the interpretation

We supplied two coordinate pairs, a weight formula, and an add-then-square
reading. Arithmetic then gave 4, 2, and 0 on three preparations with
equal separate weights. What would make that a claim about an apparatus?

We would have to say how a physical preparation corresponds to each pair,
what procedure reads a weight, which operation combines preparations,
and how the apparatus's reported numbers correspond to this formula.
Those correspondences need evidence. They are not supplied by the proof.

For example, suppose an experimenter proposes that preparing A, B, and C
under a fixed protocol will yield those three reported values after
calibration. An observed disagreement would require checking the protocol,
calibration, and proposed correspondence. It would not make 1 + 1 = 2
false. This paragraph proposes a test structure; it reports no experiment.

Keep three claims apart:

1. The recorded weights do not determine the displayed readout. The
   conflicting table rows establish that finite information gap.
2. The admitted two-coordinate model realizes the gap. Its formula
   establishes these readings under those arithmetic choices.
3. A physical system follows that model. This needs an independently
   justified measurement connection, which this route has not built.

The research's general claim about one particular formula at every
loop size remains conditional. Its later transport and native-source
rows remain open. Teaching the checked finite examples does not close
those rows or make the model a uniquely selected description of reality.
