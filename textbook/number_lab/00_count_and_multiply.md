# 00. Count and multiply

Put two counters in a left tray and three in a right tray. Counting all
the counters gives five. We write **2 + 3 = 5**: the plus sign means add
the counts, and the equals sign says the two expressions have the same
value. Zero, written **0**, records no counters in a tray.

Now make three copies of a two-counter packet. The total is
**2 + 2 + 2 = 6**. Multiplication is a compact way to write this repeated
addition: **3 × 2 = 6**, read “three times two.” Predict the total for
four copies of a two-counter packet before counting them.

The answer is eight: 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8. Later we will use multiplication
on signed numbers too; Lesson 03 introduces the extra rules needed there.

A *weight table* assigns a count to each listed position. For our two
trays the table is `(2, 3)`, and its total is 5. The order matters:
the first entry belongs to the left tray, the second to the right.
We have chosen both the positions and what their numbers mean.

List each position once when it represents one tray. A raw list such as
`[left, left, right]` counts the left tray twice and gives 2 + 2 + 3 = 7.
The source distinguishes this list total from its duplicate-free
carrier policy. A calculation can be correct for a list that is the
wrong record of your intended objects.
