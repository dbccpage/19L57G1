# 12. How many of this size?

You have 3/4 of a sheet. One standard piece is 1/2 of a sheet.
Division asks how many standard pieces your amount represents.

**Predict:** does **(3/4) ÷ (1/2)** give less than one, exactly one,
or more than one standard piece?

<details><summary>Check your prediction</summary>

More than one: 3/4 contains one half-sheet plus another quarter-sheet.
That last quarter is half a standard piece, so the answer is **3/2**
standard pieces. Check by multiplying: **3/2 × 1/2 = 3/4**.

</details>

For a positive dividing fraction, multiply by its **reciprocal**:
exchange its numerator and denominator. Thus dividing by 1/2 means
multiplying by 2/1. The original divisor times its reciprocal is one.
Dividing by a positive amount smaller than one can increase the written
number because you are counting smaller units.

The answer 3/2 here is a ratio to a chosen unit, not a probability.
Always keep what a number counts with the number.

### Zero needs a separate decision

An amount zero divided by a positive piece size gives zero pieces.
Dividing by a zero piece size supplies no reciprocal and no quotient
in this toolkit. Writing zero as 0/4 does not make it a valid divisor.
The zero is in its value, even though its own denominator is positive.

**Change one thing:** replace the available amount by 1/3 of a sheet
and the standard piece by 1/6. What quotient and multiplication check result?

<details><summary>Check the changed example</summary>

The quotient is **2**: **1/3 × 6/1 = 2**. Checking gives
**2 × 1/6 = 1/3**.

</details>
