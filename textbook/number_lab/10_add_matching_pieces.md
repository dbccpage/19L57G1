# 10. Add matching pieces

One part contains 1/4 of a whole sheet; another contains 2/4 of that same
whole. Combining them gives three quarter-pieces: **1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4**.
We add the piece counts while keeping their shared piece size.

**Predict:** can 1/2 + 1/4 be found by adding both top numbers and
both bottom numbers, giving 2/6?

<details><summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. First use matching pieces: 1/2 is 2/4. Then
**2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4**. The denominator names the piece size; it is not
another count of pieces to add.

</details>

A **common denominator** gives both fractions the same piece size.
For 1/3 and 1/4, twelfths work:

| Original share | Same value in twelfths |
|---|---|
| 1/3 | 4/12 |
| 1/4 | 3/12 |

Their sum is **7/12**. Multiplying the two original denominators always
supplies a common denominator here; sometimes a smaller one also works.
For 1/2 and 1/4, quarters suffice even though eighths would work too.

**Change one thing:** add 3/4 and 1/2. Can the answer exceed one?

<details><summary>Check the changed example</summary>

Yes. **3/4 + 2/4 = 5/4**, or one whole and one quarter. Fractions
can describe quantities greater than one. A probability model adds a
separate rule requiring each chance to be at most one.

</details>

For a numerical difference such as 5/8 − 1/8, remove matching pieces:
four eighths remain, giving 4/8 = 1/2. Signed results, when more is
removed than supplied, belong to the separate signed-step lesson.
