# Boundary and swap practice

Work without the [answers](ANSWERS.md) or revealed desk results first.
Give a reason, not just a yes/no answer.

1. At genesis, neither side has a mark. In reading O, are the two side
   profiles equal? What supplies the difference? Does the same answer
   hold in U?
2. Two presentations carry the label `b`. Compare admitting a fresh
   occurrence twice with admitting by label twice. In which case does
   the requirement concerning two retained marks have a subject?
3. A report family contains `(yes, yes)`, `(no, no)`, and `(no, yes)`.
   Does the swap preserve every report? Exhibit a witness. Does repeating
   the first report change the verdict?
4. Remove the unequal-answer report from question 3. Then remove both
   remaining reports. What happens at each step, and why?
5. In the side-free model, labels are `[b, a]`, permutations are permitted,
   and labels are kept. List the permitted automorphisms. Now forget the
   labels: does the separation verdict change?
6. Labels are `[a, a, b]`, permutations are permitted, and labels are kept.
   Can the first two marks be separated by the stated requirement? Can
   mark 0 be separated from mark 2? Explain why three marks need more
   care than a single two-answer report.
7. Return to the fixed `[a, a]` candidate menu. Someone finds that the
   identity-only candidate satisfies at cost 1, then reports “a new
   distinguishing measurement was proved” and “orientation always costs
   one.” Identify what each conclusion adds beyond the calculation.
8. A real desk keeps two physical objects in trays labelled 0 and 1.
   It forbids renaming the trays. What observation could challenge the
   claim that this rule alone guarantees correct physical identification?
   Would that observation refute the formal permutation theorem?

For a later retention check, put this sheet away and invent another
three-mark example with two repeated labels. Explain which marks a
label-preserving permutation can exchange. This is a proposed exercise,
not a record of a reader completing it.
