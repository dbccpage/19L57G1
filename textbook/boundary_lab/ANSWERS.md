# Boundary and swap practice: answers

Try [the practice](PRACTICE.md) first.

1. O profiles differ through the admitted names near and far; the
   placement lists are both empty. U has no internal side names, and its
   genesis profiles agree. Equal profiles do not identify the sides.
2. Fresh occurrences give `[b, b]`, two identities with one repeated
   label. Label-keyed admissions give `[b]`, so there is no pair of
   retained marks to test in that case. Neither policy is forced by the
   spelling `b`.
3. No. `(no, yes)` changes under exchange and witnesses failure. Repeating
   `(yes, yes)` supplies no unequal answer and cannot remove that witness.
4. The two equal-answer reports both pass, so the swap survives. The empty
   family also passes: there is no report that violates preservation.
5. Only identity preserves `[b, a]`; swap gives `[a, b]`. If labels are
   forgotten, identity and swap preserve the mark-count key. The two
   marks are separated in the first setting and not in the second.
6. The automorphisms are identity `[0,1,2]` and the exchange `[1,0,2]`.
   The first two marks are not separated. Mark 0 is separated from mark 2
   because no label-preserving permutation carries an `a` to the `b`
   position. The two-answer model stores a selected pair's two answers;
   it is not the complete three-mark permutation model.
7. The calculation establishes satisfaction by a changed permission
   family, not a measured physical feature. Its price counts changed
   capability settings in a fixed finite eligible menu. A general price
   claim needs a specified candidate space, measure, and justified
   comparison; one example's asymmetric-witness count supplies no such
   bridge. Excluding identity-only solutions leaves no satisfying
   eligible row in this menu.
8. Track the objects independently during a controlled exchange or
   transfer, then check whether the records still identify the intended
   objects. A failed association challenges the application or procedure.
   It does not refute a theorem about the declared record permutations.
   Physical identification needs a supported record-to-object connection.
