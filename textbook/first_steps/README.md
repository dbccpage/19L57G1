# First steps: what can a question reveal?

This is a beginner reading route outside the sealed
derivation. The examples are supplied teaching models. Their objects,
questions, answer tables, and rules are declared here; they are not
derived from the opaque mark.

Start with three cards. Give a small machine one question. Predict which
cards it can tell apart. Then change one thing and try again.

No previous mathematics, philosophy, coding, or proof-checker experience
is assumed. Take as much time as you need. The first reading route is
designed for a short sitting; its actual learning time still needs novice
testing.

## What we supplied

We supplied exactly three cards, named A, B, and C:

- A is blue and circular.
- B is blue and triangular.
- C is amber and triangular.

We supplied two yes-or-no questions: **blue?** and **triangle?** We also
supplied their answer tables. These are the whole example. A learner can
see more on the page than the machine has been instructed to read.

An **admission** is something explicitly added under a declared rule. In
this teaching model, the list of cards and the answer tables are supplied
at the beginning. Selecting a question makes its answers available to the
machine. It does not demonstrate a discovery of color or shape from the
opaque mark.

## Source map and limits

The main reading route uses everyday words. The precise correspondences
are collected in [SOURCE_MAP.md](SOURCE_MAP.md). It connects the examples
to the existing definitions of contexts, profiles, compression, signatures,
and factorization. The executable check also replays a unary-form encoding
through the sealed Python kernel.

Each question here has an explicitly supplied answer for each of three
cards. In the sealed kernel, a failed positive derivability check means
that the instance is not derivable under the declared rules; it does not
by itself prove a negative fact about a physical object. The source map
keeps those meanings separate.

A reader's successful predictions concern this model. Applying the idea
to a real measuring device requires checking what its answers track and
how reliable the measurements are. The final lesson gives a small way to
ask that next question.

## Teaching goal and present evidence

The intended outcome is that a new reader can make and explain predictions
in an unfamiliar example. Passing software checks establishes the declared
calculations; comprehension requires reader feedback and transfer tasks.
No novice learning result is claimed for this route yet.

Continue to the [Reasoning lab](../reasoning_lab/README.md) to practice
rules, functions, if-then claims, witnesses, and counterexamples on these
same cards. Then the [Search lab](../search_lab/README.md) starts a new
ledger and follows a separation demand through proposed repairs, costs,
ties, and independently replayed events.
