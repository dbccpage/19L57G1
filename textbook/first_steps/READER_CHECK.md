# Reader check: explain a new example

[First steps](README.md)

This is a learning check, not a race. The proposed entrance target is a
short sitting of about twenty minutes; actual reading and task time
should be recorded. It has not been established that novices will meet
that target.

Use your own words. A correct guess without a reason gives us less
information about what the explanation helped you understand. The separate
answer guide is for checking after you finish.

We supply four samples and two questions:

| Sample | lamp on? | clicks? |
|---|---|---|
| J | Yes | Yes |
| K | No | Yes |
| L | Yes | No |
| M | Yes | Yes |

1. A machine records only **clicks?**. Someone changes J's display name
   to **River**. Does this change its recorded answer? State which
   assumption you used.
2. Still recording only **clicks?**, give every observational group.
   Explain whether J and K are separate entries in the supplied list
   even if their recorded answers agree.
3. The machine now records both questions. Give a test witnessing a
   separation between J and K, with its two answers. Give one pair
   that still shares a pattern.
4. A person says, “Adding a question can never lose an old distinction.”
   State the condition under which the reasoning in Lesson 03 supports
   this claim. Then describe a replacement of one question by the other
   that loses a distinction in this table.
5. Return to recording only **clicks?**. Someone asks for **lamp on?**.
   Can one rule always recover the requested answer for these samples?
   Give a rule covering every recorded group or a particular pair that
   blocks recovery.

## Transfer question

A new machine records no questions. What observation would you need to
supply to distinguish two of its admitted items? Explain what the machine
would have to receive, and why giving the items different names alone
would not perform that task under its current rules.

## Reader feedback

- Time spent on the lessons and on this check, recorded separately.
- A word, sentence, or diagram that stopped you, if any.
- One prediction you changed and the reason you changed it.
- One question the lessons have not answered for you.

Keep the responses as observations about this reader and this edition.
They are not a proof about every teenager or an established learning-time
guarantee. See [READER_CHECK_ANSWERS.md](READER_CHECK_ANSWERS.md) afterward.
