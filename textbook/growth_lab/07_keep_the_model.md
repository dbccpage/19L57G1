# 07. Keep the claim with its model

[Previous](06_test_the_budget_rule.md) · [Growth lab](README.md) · [Practice](PRACTICE.md)

## Predict first

One research result says growth adds n demands. A related experiment
sometimes adds only one. Must one of them be wrong?

## Compare the inputs before comparing conclusions

The n-count theorem uses `total`, a fresh item, and a world without views.
Our earlier fully separated four-item example had views. It added just
one demand, 4/3. The theorem's no-views condition was not satisfied.

The related `two_sided_commitment` tree grows at demand-free states in a
different finite response experiment. Its ledger explicitly distinguishes
its observed counts from the sibling's viewless counting theorem.
Its “at most one” general explanation is recorded there as a conjecture;
this route does not promote that explanation to a theorem.

There is another distinction: the accounting service removes entries,
whereas a represented-world repair would need to change observations
through its actual rules. A matching number is not a proof those two
machines implement each other.

## Carry a compact claim record

For a result you want to use, state:

- the held items and possible arrivals;
- the commitment and observation rules;
- the permitted changes and any service assumption;
- the exact conclusion, horizon, and kind of evidence.

To apply the model to real record keeping, additionally specify how item
identity, observations, and successful repairs are measured. A new item
with an unexpected observation pattern may challenge that correspondence.
It does not make the theorem false under its original definitions.
