## Core Theorems & Implementations

### 1. Canonical Symplectic Form Preservation over F_p^2
On the 2D discrete phase_space $\mathbb{F}_p^2$, the canonical bilinear form is defined by:
$$\omega(u, v) = (u_x \cdot v_z - u_z \cdot v_x) \pmod p$$
For any $2 \times 2$ matrix $M = \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$:
$$\omega(M u, M v) \equiv \det(M) \cdot \omega(u, v) \pmod p$$
Therefore, $M$ preserves the symplectic form if and only if $\det(M) \equiv 1 \pmod p$, establishing the isomorphism $\mathrm{Sp}(2, \mathbb{F}_p) \cong \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{F}_p)$.

### 2. Single-Carrier Projective Clifford Group Quotient Sp(2, F_2) =~ S_3
In characteristic 2, the single-carrier projective Clifford group quotients to $\mathrm{Sp}(2, \mathbb{F}_2)$, generated by the discrete Hadamard and S operations:
$$H = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \quad S = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
This group has order 6 and consists of:
- **Identity**: $I = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ (order 1)
- **Order-2 Reflections**: $H, S, HSH = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$
- **Order-3 Rotations**: $SH = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, HS = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$

The package establishes:
- All 6 matrices have determinant $1 \pmod 2$.
- All 6 matrices are mutually distinct and preserve the symplectic form.
- The action on the 3 non-zero vectors of $\mathbb{F}_2^2$ is faithful, providing an explicit permutation isomorphism to the symmetric group $S_3$.

### 3. Single-Carrier Projective Clifford Group Quotient Sp(2, F_3) =~ SL(2, F_3)
In dimension $p = 3$ (qutrit), the projective Clifford group quotients to $\mathrm{Sp}(2, \mathbb{F}_3) \cong \mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{F}_3)$ of order:
$$|\mathrm{SL}(2, \mathbb{F}_3)| = 3(3^2 - 1) = 24$$
Generated by $H_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and $S_3 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.
The order spectrum across the 24 elements is:
- 1 element of order 1 ($I$)
- 1 element of order 2 ($-I$)
- 8 elements of order 3
- 6 elements of order 4
- 8 elements of order 6

### 4. Eastin-Knill Finite Quotient Obstruction
For non_classical error-correcting codes, logical operations implemented transversally preserve the stabilizer partition and quotient to a subgroup of $\mathrm{Sp}(2n, \mathbb{F}_p)$.
Because $\mathrm{Sp}(2n, \mathbb{F}_p)$ is a strictly finite group (order 6 for $n=1, p=2$; order 24 for $n=1, p=3$), the group generated by transversal operations is discrete and finite.
Consequently, transversal operations cannot form a dense subgroup of the continuous Lie group $\mathrm{SU}(d)$ (which has dimension $d^2 - 1 \ge 3$). Dense covering of state carrier operations requires non-transversal resources such as magic state injection.
