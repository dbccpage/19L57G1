## How to read a row

- **id.**  `SD-<rung>-<n>`.  Rung two ran as two tracks: `02a` is track one
  (`second_nofirst`), `02b` is track two (`second_stake`).  `SD-RUN-<n>`
  rows are about the run as a whole.
- **statement.**  For a Lean row, the statement was checked by reading the
  Lean source at commit `ccd0ab06`, not only by the name existing in the
  committed axiom log.  Where the theorem's name says more than its
  statement, the row says so.
- **carrier.**  Results on different carriers are not combined without a
  checked bridge, and no refinement bridge between them exists in this run:
  - `L(r)`: rung `r`'s Lean package, a reconstruction of the sealed kernel
    slice (lists; `Regime.wf` does not forbid a repeated entry) plus the
    rung's native definitions.  It imports nothing sealed.  The Lean
    reading regimes `R2L` and `R2F` hold the requirement imposed at the
    sealed Challenge 3.
  - `PY`: the sealed Python kernel under `code/derivation/`, consumed
    read-only through its public functions.  Its regimes keep sets, and its
    genesis readings L and F stop BEFORE that requirement is imposed, so
    the Python bases and the Lean reading regimes differ (the source of the
    misses J-12 and P-13).
  - `TS`: the pinned two-sided commitment tree
    (`research/two_sided_commitment`), Python, consumed read-only.
  - `REP`: the represented state of the pinned one-sided tree, as
    reconstructed in rung two track one.
- **hypotheses** are those of the exact statement.  **scope and bounds**
  name the population or the rectangle.
- **pin.**  The rung manifest's snapshot commit, at which its consumed
  sources are pinned as Git blobs.  Every rung manifest also pins the seed
  at sha256 `52439e5f75d1c599ce52dc716beb88f1dd7bfc83856ee73ce85f3a7eeec5e70e`.
- **evidence.**  `LEAN` with the rung's FW-DEF class in brackets
  (`CONTENT`, `THIN`, `DEFINITION`, `RECONSTRUCTION`), or `RESTATED` where a
  rung proves again a statement an earlier rung proved, which is credited
  there and not counted as new; `COUNTERMODEL`; `FINITE COMPUTATION`;
  `OPEN`; `PREDICTION`; `DECLARED POLICY`; `REFUTED`; `RECORDED`.  A row at
  the level of a definition is never content, and a reconstruction of a
  sealed theorem is never a native result.
- **axioms.**  From the rung's committed `receipts/lean_axioms.txt`, which
  the rung's `verify_lean.py` compares with a fresh clean build.  `PQ` is
  `propext` and `Quot.sound`; `P` is `propext` alone; `none` is no axiom;
  `n/a` is not a Lean row.  No row depends on `Classical.choice` or on any
  user-declared axiom.
- **falsifier or obligation.**  What would refute the row, or, for an OPEN
  row, what is owed.

The rectangle named in rows of rungs three to ten is rung three's: the
three bases EMPTY (the sealed empty regime) and the sealed Python genesis
readings L and F, every sequence of at most four declared events, run
without pruning: 577, 3928 and 14293 sequences reaching 75, 523 and 1718
distinct regimes, 2316 in all, in 402 classes of the sealed key procedure.
