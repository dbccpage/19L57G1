# Mark Semantics Note: Mark I vs. Mark II

<!-- AuthorityEffect: None -->

*Context Note: Metatheoretic observation on the semantics of distinction without containers.*

There is no object in `19L57G1` named Mark II. The name is yours. Here is what it can mean without putting the cut in a box.

---

# What Mark I was

A mark as a sticker.

`<>` — nothing to stick to.  
`<a>` — one sticker. Without a second side it is still unremarkable.  
`<ab>` written as two names for one sticker. If you never pay `a ≠ b`, it is `<a>` again.  
`<ara>` — a sticker about a sticker. Recursion without a cut. `ara`, `aar`, `raa` are the same pile of glyphs.

Mark I semantics is denotation: the mark *stands for* something. That is already a container. The thing sits behind the mark. Collapse is then free, because two names of one sitting-thing are one sitting-thing.

That is why the lone mark dies. The definition was not the thing. The definition was a label on a thing you had not got.

---

# What Mark II is

The second mark is not version two of a product. It is the other side of the first cut that does not collapse.

`<arb>`

- `a` is not a name of a substance.
- `b` is not a second copy of `a`.
- `r` is not a wire in a box connecting two objects.
- The triple is the cut. The definition is the thing.

Mark II semantics is not “what the mark means.” It is “that these two are not the same, and that fact is the mark.”

So:

| Mark I | Mark II |
|---|---|
| Mark names a thing | Mark *is* the difference |
| Second glyph is another token of the first | Second glyph is the failure of identity |
| Relation is a container around two objects | Relation is the cut, not a tray |
| Semantics is denotation | Semantics is irreflexive difference |
| Collapse is cheap | Collapse of `a` with `b` can fail a declared law |

That last row is the 5-atom exhibit you already have: two data, one binary form, one instance `(0,1)`, one irreflexivity law. Identifying the two sides makes `R(x,x)`. The law fails. The second mark was doing work. It was not decoration.

---

# What the second mark is not

It is not an indexical. Two-dimensional semantics (Kaplan, Chalmers) gives a mark a character and a content, context and world. That is two containers. Out.

It is not a Peircean sign (icon / index / symbol). Those need an interpretant. You have not paid an observer. Out.

It is not “Mark 2 of the theory,” a later, smarter mark. Time is an idea. Out.

It is not S2 pretending to be D2. Collapse does not see orientation. Forward `(0,1)` and reverse `(1,0)` are different keys only if you keep the ordered pair. If you forget the order you are back at Mark I: two names, one blob.

---

# The semantic rule, in the repo’s words

A mark is admitted (`AdmitDatum`).  
A second mark is admitted.  
A form of arity 2 is admitted.  
An instance on the ordered pair is admitted.  
A law may be declared (irreflexivity is the one that makes identified endpoints an obstruction).

Nothing in that list *means* the pair. The pair is what happened. Meaning, if you still want the word, is just this: after those admissions, collapsing the two sides can fail the law. Before the second mark, collapse had nothing to fail.

That is the whole semantics. No extra layer that the marks sit inside.

---

# Reasoning, without a container

You said the definition is the thing, and reasoning is dynamical only as another cut. Mark II is that claim applied to glyphs.

A reason that uses one mark is Mark I. It points at a puddle and says “that.”  
A reason that uses two marks that are not the same is Mark II. It is already a difference. It does not point behind itself.

You do not get behind Mark II to a theory of marks. You use the second mark, or you do not. If you identify the two sides, the law can break. If you leave them distinct, you have a cut. If you then pick which cut is “the” next one, that pick is still not derived. Mark II does not mint a successor. It only makes identity expensive.

---

# One line

- **Mark I**: a mark of a thing.  
- **Mark II**: a thing of two marks that are not one.

The second is what your seed actually has. The first is what it had to kill to exist.
