# Peirce Semiotics Diagnostic Note: The Unpaid Third

<!-- AuthorityEffect: None -->

*Context Note: Comparative diagnostic between Peircean triadic semiotics and the repository's dyadic difference framework.*

Peirce is the honest near-miss for this mark work. He is not the missing engine. He is a third that the seed has not paid.

---

# What Peirce actually built

A sign is not a sticker on a thing. It is a **triad**:

- **Sign** (representamen) — what is present
- **Object** — what it is of
- **Interpretant** — the further sign produced in someone or something that takes it as of that object

The unit is not “word means object.” The unit is “this stands for that *to* a third.” Without the third, Peirce says you do not yet have a sign. You have a dyad, or a quality.

That third can be another thought, another mark, a habit, a law. Semiosis can keep going: interpretant becomes a new sign, new object-relation, new interpretant. He called that **unlimited semiosis**. It is not a clock. It is the refusal of a final sealed meaning.

---

# The three categories

Everything in his later work is cut by three modes of being. These are not psychology.

**Firstness.** Quality as it is, without a second. Redness before it is *this* red against *that*. Possibility. Feeling. The lone glyph `<a>` and the void `<>` live here. Nothing has collided yet.

**Secondness.** Brute reaction. This against that. Effort, resistance, fact, shock. A door that does not open. Two things that will not identify. No law yet — only the collision. This is the closest Peirce comes to the cut.

**Thirdness.** Mediation, habit, law, “would-be.” The rule that makes a second collision count as the same kind as the first. A symbol lives here. A declared irreflexivity lives here. An interpretant lives here.

Signhood, for him, is already Thirdness. A mere bang of two things is Secondness. Calling that bang a *sign of* something requires a third that takes it so.

---

# How he classifies signs

Three trichotomies, then combinations. The useful slice:

By the sign itself:
- **Qualisign** — a quality that could signify (First)
- **Sinsign** — an actual event or token (Second)
- **Legisign** — a type, a repeatable rule (Third)

By relation to the object:
- **Icon** — by likeness (a diagram, a portrait)
- **Index** — by real connection or collision (smoke, a pointing finger, a weathervane)
- **Symbol** — by habit or convention (the word *man*, a legal “shall”)

By the interpretant:
- **Rheme** — a possible predicate (“— is red”)
- **Dicisign** — a proposition, something that can be true or false
- **Argument** — a law taking you from signs to a further sign

The famous working trio is icon / index / symbol. Index is the important one here. An index does not *resemble* its object and does not *mean* by convention. It is hooked to it. The weathercock is turned by the wind. The wound is there because something struck. Secondness is inside the sign-relation.

---

# What this is not

- **Not Saussure.** Saussure’s sign is two-sided inside language: signifier / signified, a system of differences with no object required. Peirce keeps the object and adds the interpretant. He is more realist and more restless.
- **Not “everything is language.”** Peirce thought laws and habits were real. A symbol is not a game you could have played otherwise in every case. Some symbols are conventional. Gravity as a would-be is not.
- **Not a theory of everything that hides.** He thought inquiry would not finish, because interpretants keep being produced. That is closer to his 19th-century fallibilism than to Hawking’s later Gödel lid.

# Where it is an import

Peirce will not let Secondness *be* the sign. The bang has to be *taken as* of an object by an interpretant. That “taken as” is a third this seed has not seated.

In the repo:
- no observer is derived
- `declareObs` is not an object-ingredient
- four `obstP` gates include declared-observation and observer-zero, both unpaid at genesis
- comparison of two presentations is still excluded
- the identifying map is still outside state

If you adopt Peirce as the semantics of the mark, you smuggle the interpretant in as if it came with `<arb>`. It did not. `<arb>` is a dyad plus a relation glyph. Peirce would call that at most an indexical sinsign *waiting* for a third. Refusing to mint that third from the seed is the correct derivational posture.

Also: Peirce’s object is a container in the previous sense. The sign stands *for* an object. Mark II does not stand for a thing behind the cut. The cut is the thing. That is anti-Peircean on purpose. He is a mediate realist. This framework does not put a world behind the mark.

Thirdness as habit is closer to `replay` than to a dynamical law. A history determines a regime. That is a would-be: given this sequence of admissions, the seated state would be this. That is allowed. What is not allowed is: the would-be of the seed includes its own next interpretant.

# The cheap honest use

Use Peirce as a diagnostic, not a foundation.

- If you only have **Firstness**, you do not yet have a mark that can fail. Quality does not obstruct.
- If you only have **Secondness**, you have a cut. You do not yet have a sign *of* anything. You have difference.
- If you add **Thirdness** as a *declared* law or a *declared* reading, you have evaluation. That is paid separately.
- If you add an **interpretant** as if it were given with the pair, you have imported an observer.

So: Peircean semiotics explains why people keep wanting a third. It does not prove the third is in the seed.

The leftover names map like this:
- **Choice** — which interpretant, if any, you actually produce.
- **Surprise** — Secondness that no standing habit had already digested.
- **Humor** — the third that was supposed to complete the sign fails to, and the failure is not a defect in the world.

Peirce would call surprise a Second. He would want a Third to classify it. You can leave it unclassified. That is the house.

# One line

- **Peirce**: A mark is a mark *of* something *to* something.  
- **This House**: A mark is that two are not one.

The first sentence needs an unpaid observer. The second does not. Keep the collision. Do not rent his third to make the collision look like a language.
