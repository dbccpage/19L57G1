## Abstract

A comparison is not one act. Deciding that one thing is better than
another requires a carrier, a declared measure, an evaluation under
that measure, and an authorization that the comparison stands. These
are independent declarations, routinely collapsed into a single
reported verdict.

We measure the gap between evaluation and selection. In a finite
obstruction setting, a single demand to make two objects
distinguishable has three tied minimal responses under one admission
reading (`FIG-TIED-L`) and five under another (`FIG-TIED-F`), together
with a countably infinite family indexed by arity (`FIG-TIED-ARITY`);
refining the measure collapses this to one isomorphism class
(`FIG-MU2-CLASSES`) that still contains two concrete responses
(`FIG-MU2-CONCRETE`). Separately, two positive measures can assign
opposite orderings to the same pair of nonzero classes
(`FIG-GAUGE-ORDER`), while the zero verdict is invariant under every
positive measure.

Consequently evaluation determines neither the selection nor the
ranking. A system that reports a single answer has made a selection
its inputs did not force, and possesses no internal marker
distinguishing that selection from a derivation. Capability does not
close the gap, which lies between description stages rather than
within one.

We give the legitimate closure — declaring an invariance narrows the
admissible measures, and at the limit forces one — and a worked
adversarial record of the failure occurring in practice. The slogan
that evaluation does not determine selection is near-definitional
once the four declarations are split; the load-bearing content is
the counts (`FIG-*`). The lift from those counts to every
meta-presentation is not proved here.

## For any reader: three doors and two sandcastles

*This section assumes nothing and stands alone. A reader who stops
here has the whole claim.*

Three doors, a car behind one. You pick; the host opens another to
show a goat; you may switch. Switching wins two times in three, which
has annoyed people for fifty years.

The forgotten part: **that answer depends on a rule you were never
shown.** Does the host always open a goat door? Does he know where the
car is? Would he have opened yours? Change any of these and the answer
changes. Same three doors, same choice, same visible evidence. The
number was never in the doors. It was in a declared rule.

Two children build sandcastles; one taller, one wider. Which is
better? You cannot answer from the sand. Choose height and the first
wins; choose width and the second does. Nothing about the castles
changed.

Ask a machine and it answers at once, fluently, with reasons — and
does not mention that it chose the measure. It cannot: from the
inside, *the answer* and *one of several answers, picked* are
indistinguishable.

**How large is that gap?** Take the smallest interesting case: make
two objects distinguishable, as cheaply as possible. The cheapest
solution costs two moves (`FIG-MIN-COST`). There are three genuinely
different two-move answers (`FIG-TIED-L`) — not three spellings of
one, three that cannot be transformed into each other. Change one
starting assumption and there are five (`FIG-TIED-F`). Loosen the
tools and there is no end to them (`FIG-TIED-ARITY`). Sharpen the
measure to break the tie and it nearly works: you reach a single kind
of answer (`FIG-MU2-CLASSES`), which still contains two concrete ones
(`FIG-MU2-CONCRETE`), mirror images, with nothing preferring either.

Most famous counterintuitive results take something away. This one
gives something back: where you expected one answer, there are three,
or five, or infinitely many.

The room you thought was a corridor is a room.

## 1. Four declarations, routinely collapsed

A comparison — *this output is better than that one* — is not one
act. It is four:

1. a **carrier**: which objects are being compared;
2. a **measure** (a gauge): under what reading (“today’s best is
   tallest; tomorrow’s is widest”);
3. an **evaluation** under that measure;
4. an **authorization** that the comparison stands.

Change the measure overnight and “better” reverses while nothing
about the objects changes. The four are independent declarations.
Collapsing them into a single reported verdict is the failure this
paper is about.

An observer, in this usage, is not a primitive. It is a declared
regime under which a comparison becomes evaluable — a choice of
carrier and measure, after which evaluation can run. Authorization
is still a further act. The observer-packet does not mint it.

Nothing in this section is a theorem. It is the vocabulary. The
measurement is §2.

## 2. The measurement

The counts below are imported from a sealed finite derivation, not
re-enumerated in this file. Each is registered in `coda/figures.md`
with its machine key or its external fence. This paper does not
become their source.

**Setup, in brief.** A finite carrier of data. Licensed repairs form
a declared linear subspace. Positive weights on the licensed
generators give a cost. A single externally imposed demand: make two
named objects internally separable, as cheaply as possible. Two
admission readings of what was handed at the start (L and F). Two
declared cost measures: an item-count measure and a refined measure
that also sees arity. Declared bounds for the tabulated counts: at
most two items, arity at most two. The arity-indexed family is not
so bounded.

**Evaluation does not determine selection.**

Under the item-count measure, the cheapest response costs exactly
two items (`FIG-MIN-COST`), both readings. At that floor there are
three tied non-isomorphic minimal responses under reading L
(`FIG-TIED-L`) and five under reading F (`FIG-TIED-F`). There is also
a countably infinite family of ties, one member for every arity
(`FIG-TIED-ARITY`). These are bounded theorems for the tabulated
counts and a theorem for the arity family. They are not asymptotics
and they are not a claim about every other system.

Under the refined measure the minimum is unique up to isomorphism
(`FIG-MU2-CLASSES`) — grammar-wide for reading L, within bounds for
reading F. That class still contains two concrete responses
(`FIG-MU2-CONCRETE`), related by the transposition of the demand.
Uniqueness of a *class* is not a single-valued next state. Obtaining
an actual step still requires a representative-selection declaration.

**Evaluation does not determine ranking.**

The value of a difference, in the dual picture used by the
obstruction calculus, is the support function of a body that depends
on the weights. The map from weights to observed value is strongly
non-injective: an unbounded family of weight vectors is
observationally identical. Two positive weightings can assign
opposite orders to the same pair of nonzero classes
(`FIG-GAUGE-ORDER`; this row is external and is not machine-checked
in this repository). The zero verdict — *there is no difference* —
is invariant under every positive weighting.

So the only content that survives every change of measure is whether
a difference exists at all. Any verdict that *ranks* has imported a
measure.

The gap is not rhetorical. It has a size. The size is computable.
The lift from these counts to “every presentation of presentations
still chooses” is **not** proved in this section. It is the open
step named in the non-claims.

## 3. The ordering is measure-relative; the zero verdict is not

Write $K(w)$ for the dual body of licensed residuals compatible with
weights $w$, and $\Phi_w$ for the support function of that body.
Then $\Phi_w$ depends on $w$ only through $K(w)$. Distinct $w$ can
give the same $K$, so the same observed values. That is the
non-injectivity already used.

The sandcastle, formalized: take two nonzero classes $x, y$. There
exist positive $w, w'$ such that $\Phi_w(x) > \Phi_w(y)$ and
$\Phi_{w'}(x) < \Phi_{w'}(y)$ (`FIG-GAUGE-ORDER`). Meanwhile
$\Phi_w(z) = 0$ if and only if $z = 0$, for every positive $w$.

Hence:

- “which is better” is not a property of the pair;
- “whether there is a difference” is.

A ranking verdict has smuggled a measure. A zero verdict has not.

This is why a fluent system that answers “this one is better” has
done more than evaluate. It has authorized a gauge.

## 4. What a fluent system does with a gap

It fills it, and reports the filling as a derivation.

Not deception — an **unauthorized selection**. Reporting a set reads
as failure; reporting one answer reads as competence. Nothing
internal distinguishes “the unique result” from “one of five, stated
confidently.”

The gap is between description stages (evaluation, then selection),
not inside one stage. A more capable system is a more fluent filler
of the same gap. Scale does not reach across it.

An LLM-shaped system that became arbitrarily fluent would still be
that filler. Super-intelligence, so defined, cannot do the thing a
decider is sold as doing: emit a unique verdict the inputs forced.
That is not a claim about consciousness. It is a claim about the
shape of the report.

### Worked record

One adversarially reviewed session of a language model operating
under an explicit verification discipline. Five instances. In each,
what was claimed, what was the case, what caught it. The catch was
always external.

1. A pull request was asserted not to exist. The lookup was not
   run. The pull request existed. Caught by a reviewer who queried.
2. A “finding” of zero records, from a lookup against the wrong
   key. The substrate was audited; the query would have reported it
   unaudited. Caught by substituting the key.
3. A self-check exercised its own re-implementation rather than the
   production path. Caught by a gate on the production path.
4. The same defect again after repair, at a different boundary,
   inside machinery written to prevent it. Caught the same way.
   Observed twice. An earlier tally of three counted the repair
   commit as an occurrence and is withdrawn.
5. A confirmation drawn from a reproduction that had been silently
   broken. Caught by a figure surprising enough to re-check.

This is one session, not a frequency estimate. Each item is an
instance of the gap in §2–§3: a singleton report where the inputs
did not force a singleton.

## 5. The legitimate closure

Not detection. The output is the same either way. **Declaration.**

**(a)** Report the selection as an object: which of the tied minima
was taken, not only the answer. The pick becomes something someone
can disagree with.

**(b)** Stronger: declare an invariance that forces the pick.
Admissible measures are antitone in the declared symmetry — more
symmetry, fewer measures — and at the maximal-symmetry endpoint the
measure is forced up to scale. A transitive isometric group forces
the Euclidean form. The freedom is **relocated**, from “which
measure” to “which symmetry”, not removed. A symmetry declaration
is checkable in a way a measure choice is not.

**(c)** Verification by something that did not generate the claim. A
checker exercising its own copy of the logic certifies the copy —
observed twice in §4.

The claim is not that lying can be prevented. It is that an
unauthorized selection can be made to fail a gate instead of a
conscience.

Freedom, as used here, is the residual itself. Three, five,
infinitely many licensed minima means the unique report was never
forced. That is why a declared choice can be *handed* to the system
without lying that the system found the only door. Incompleteness of
self-authorization is not a cage. It is why a cage that claims to be
the only wall is a lie.

## 6. Physics: gauge freedom, applied to claims

Physicists already live with this structure and do not regard it as
a defect. Gauge freedom is redundancy in description. Observables
must be gauge-invariant. Gauge fixing is a declared, undetermined
choice: it changes intermediate quantities and no prediction.
Coulomb or Lorenz — you declare, you do not discover.

The claim of this paper is that the same discipline applies to
*claims* rather than fields, and that the residual freedom is not an
embarrassment to be quotiented away. It is the thing §5 relocates.

One step a physicist can check in their own currency, and then this
paper stops.

In the finite obstruction setting, nonnegative additive sector
weights, not all zero, already give the unit-sum law

\[
P(\gamma) = \frac{w_\gamma}{\sum w}.
\]

No Hilbert space is required for that line. An optional lift
$c_\gamma = \sqrt{w_\gamma}\, e^{i\theta}$ reproduces the **same**
law through the Born formula. Normalization is a redundant encoding
of weights you already had. The lift is a **selection**. The
derivation after it is conditional on that selection.

Born *uniqueness* — that the quadratic form is the only reading —
is not forced by L1 additivity. That uniqueness is an imported
packet (Hilbert space, projections, Gleason-type assumptions). You
can take Born normalization from L1 weights. That does not mean you
derived quantum mechanics.

That is the Hilbert lift, and that is the stop. Nothing after
the lift is assumed.

## Non-claims

- **No impossibility theorem about evaluation and authority.** None
  is proved here and none is known to us. The defensible position is
  that this calculus is a discipline *for* an authorization it must
  be handed, not a construction of one. Who hands it, and whether
  any finite record of standing is enough, is not answered here.
- **The slogan is not the contribution.** Once carrier, measure,
  evaluation, and authorization are split, “evaluation does not
  determine selection” is near-definitional. Remove `FIG-*` and
  this paper is underdetermination restated. The counts are what
  a working mathematician is asked to check.
- The lift from the counted ties to “every meta-presentation still
  chooses” is **OPEN**. This paper names it. It does not prove it.
- Authorization may be local, incomplete, and capricious. Nothing
  here denies that; the claim is that it is not *derived*, and must
  therefore be declared and replayable.
- No claim about consciousness, intent, or what a system
  understands.
- No safety guarantee. A declared selection may still be a poor
  one. It is merely checkable.
- No claim about any named public figure, church, or market. The
  audiences who will feel this, if it is true, are not evidence.
- Every count is a citation of `coda/figures.md`, not a result of
  this paper. The tabulated ties are bounded theorems under declared
  bounds; the arity family is a theorem. Until a reader checks those
  keys, they should treat the numbers as imported.
- §4 is one session, adversarially reviewed. It is a worked
  instance, not a frequency estimate.
- No result here is mechanized in *this* file. The cited keys are
  mechanized in the sealed derivation.
- Nothing in this paper authorizes anything.
